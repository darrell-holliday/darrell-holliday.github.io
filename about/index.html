About

I’m Darrell. I used to close. Now I coach the ask.

I’m a Sales Coach for online entrepreneurs. I help people who sell their own offer get clear on it, talk to the right people and ask without sounding salesy.

Where I come from

I grew up in College Park, Maryland, and studied philosophy at Arizona State. Philosophy turned out to be good training for sales: ask a better question, then listen to the whole answer.

My first business job was growth marketing at a software company. I worked on product launches, funnels, email, an affiliate program and the user community, which is where I learned how offers are built and how people find them.

Then I moved into sales. I spent years on high-ticket calls over Zoom for software, education and coaching companies, and closed $12M+ along the way. I trained setters, mentored newer closers and wrote scripts teams used. On the side I ran a small consultancy helping people with offers, funnels and launches.

Why I coach

On sales calls I kept meeting the same person: someone smart, with a good idea, who had bought the course and the tools but had never asked anyone to buy. The skill that made me a good closer, understanding why someone showed up and helping them make a clear decision, turned out to be what those people needed from a coach.

Since then I’ve coached 1,000+ people building online businesses, in one-on-one sessions, daily live office hours and hands-on AI workshops, and I’ve led and trained other coaches.

I’m independent now. I work one-on-one as a Sales Coach and run small live workshops on asking, from Thailand, on US Eastern hours. Many of the people I work with build with AI, and I’m happy to talk tools, but the ask is the job.

What I believe about this work

  • Most people don’t have a conversion problem. They have an ask problem. Content gets attention, conversations create opportunities, and asking is the step most people skip.
  • Recommending the right thing isn’t salesy. Hiding it is.
  • Ten names on paper beat a perfect avatar in your head.
  • Count actions, not outcomes. You control the reps: messages sent, calls held, follow-ups done.
  • Ready, fire, aim, and give yourself some grace. You learn by doing, and beginners deserve patience.

Outside of work

Philosophy, martial arts, lifting and chess. All four reward the same thing: showing up and doing the boring rep again.
