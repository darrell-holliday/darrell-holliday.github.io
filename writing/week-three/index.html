Most people quit in week three. Here’s how I coach them through it.

Week one is exciting. New plan, new tools, big energy.

Week two is busy. Lots of setup. It still feels like progress.

Week three is where people disappear.

The novelty is gone. The first post got four likes. The first sales conversation went nowhere. Life shows up. And the little voice says "maybe this isn’t for me."

I’ve seen this pattern often. Here’s what I do about it.

I tell them it’s coming. On day one. "Around week three, this is going to feel pointless. That’s normal. That’s the part where most people quit, and it’s the part where the business actually gets built." When it hits, they recognize it instead of believing it.

I shrink the goal. Nobody needs a 12-step plan in week three. They need one thing to finish this week. One offer page. Five conversations. One post. Small enough that they can’t talk themselves out of it.

I go back to why they started. Not in a fluffy way. I ask what life looks like in six months if this works, and what it looks like if they stop now. That used to be a sales technique for me. Now it’s how I keep people from quitting on themselves.

I look for proof, not perfection. Did someone reply? Did anyone ask a question? Did they get a "not now" instead of silence? That’s movement. I point it out, because they usually won’t.

Coaching isn’t about having better information than the internet. The information is free. Coaching is about getting someone through the boring middle, where most people stop.

If you’re in week three of something right now, keep going. Shrink the goal. Finish one thing this week.
