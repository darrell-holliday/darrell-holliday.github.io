Have these conversations before you add another AI tool

If your week is full of new tools and empty of conversations, the business is not being built. The stack is.

I like AI. I use it. I also don’t start there with a client who isn’t selling yet. Sales still come from a person hearing an offer and deciding to buy. The tools can wait a week.

Here are the conversations I want you to have before you add another AI tool.

The offer conversation, with yourself. Out loud, not in a doc you keep rewriting. Who is this for? What changes for them? Why would they do it this month? What’s the next step? If any of those answers are fuzzy, that’s the job. Not a new model.

The five-name conversation. Write down five people you can already reach who might be the buyer, or who know the buyer. If you cannot name five, your offer is probably aimed at “people online,” which is not a market. Tighten it until names appear.

The direct ask. Message or call those five and say what you’re offering, in plain words, and ask if they want to talk about it. Not “I’ve been working on something, would love your feedback.” That’s a way to hide. Ask.

The debrief. After each conversation, write down what they repeated, where they hesitated, and what they asked next. Those are the words that belong in the offer. They are better than anything a prompt will invent.

The follow-up. Most first conversations do not end in a yes. The sale, when it happens, is often in the second or third message, the one most people never send. Write that follow-up yourself, once. You can let AI draft the next ones after you know what a good one sounds like.

That’s a week of work. It does not require a new subscription. When you’ve had those conversations, you’ll know what to automate: the follow-up you were already willing to send, the outline you already believe, the notes you already take. Until then, another tool is a way to avoid the part that feels like rejection.

Do the conversations this week. Add the tool after you have something worth repeating.
