Your first $1,000 online is an offer problem, not a traffic problem

When someone tells me they need more traffic, I ask one question.

"If 10 of the right people saw your offer today, how many would buy?"

Usually there’s a long pause. Then: "I don’t know."

That’s the problem. Not traffic.

When you’re starting out, you don’t need thousands of visitors. You need a small number of people who read your offer and think "that’s exactly me." If you can’t get that reaction from 10 people you know, 10,000 strangers won’t fix it.

Here’s what I look at first with a new business:

Who exactly is it for? "Entrepreneurs" is not an answer. "Fitness coaches who sell 1:1 and want a group program" is an answer.

What changes for them? Not features. The before and after, in their words.

Why now? What makes this worth paying for this month instead of someday?

Why you? One honest reason. A result, a story, a skill. It doesn’t have to be huge. It has to be true.

What’s the next step? One. Apply, buy the thing, or join the list. Not three options.

Get those five right and your first sales usually come from people you can already reach: your network, your DMs, a few honest posts about what you’re building.

Then you earn the right to worry about traffic.

I’ve watched people spend months on ads and funnels for an offer nobody had said yes to yet. I’ve also watched people make their first few thousand dollars with a Google Doc and a calendar link. The second group had a clear offer.

Start there.
